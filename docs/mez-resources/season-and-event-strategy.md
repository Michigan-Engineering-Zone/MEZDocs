# Season & Event Strategy

## Planning Ahead

- Success at the event starts with a season-long strategy
- Season strategy turns into event strategy and match-by-match strategy
- Your build season strategy is derived from your team's goals
- Decide on what you want your team to accomplish, and pick a robot that will do this, considering your resources

## Big Questions

- Tank or swerve?
- Kitbot, Everybot, vendor bot, or custom?
- Golden rule: almost no one ever regrets building too little robot
- Frustrating seasons, 99% of the time, happen from over-commitment
- Regardless of your robot, practice time will make the biggest difference

## Season Milestones

- Milestone list is done early this year
- It includes build, coding, and wiring
- There are two weeks built in for snow days
- If you keep up with it, you'll have a successful season

## Robot Reliability

- Generally the most important thing at an event
- As you build, focus on quality
  - Screws tight; loc-tited where necessary
  - Every electrical connection secure and tug-tested
- The best way to determine your robot's failure points is to finish early and practice with it.

## Leading Up to the Event

- Determine your drive team in advance
  - Understand the trade-offs for rotating drive team
- Make sure you understand the game rules and penalties
- Do practice matches and train for different situations
- Have a plan for how to contribute in each match, whether you're playing offense or defense

## At the Event

- Go into the event with a goal
- Try to get inspected early
- Try to play practice matches if you can
- Have a pre-match checklist for robot maintenance
- Have a strategy for each match based on your goal

## At the Event

- Communicate with alliance partners each match
- Adjust your strategy from match-to-match based on situation
- If you're having a technical problem, ask for help!
- Review your match footage and see what you can improve
  - [thebluealliance.com](https://www.thebluealliance.com)

## Scouting

- Not just for alliance captains, although important for that
- Your knowledge will impress teams considering picking you
- You can find tactics to use in matches to help you win
  - Defending certain robots certain ways
  - Scoring in certain ways based on match situation
- So, how do we scout?

## Scouting

- Pit scouting
- Match scouting
- Vibe scouting
- Getting data without scouting

## Pit Scouting

- Only so useful
- There are a lot of annoying pit scouts
  - Be courteous, honest, and brief
  - Say more if they're asking good questions
- Some good questions you can ask:
  - Ask about robot characteristics ("Can you go under the trench?")
  - What is your preferred strategy for teleop and auto? Do you have backup strategies?
  - What's the most useful thing a partner can do to help you succeed in a match?
  - Is there anything else you like from alliance partners?

## Vibe Scouting

- Every time you interact with a team, you're sharing data with each other
- If a team is accommodating and easy to work with, that matters
  - The same goes for you
- Even without recording data, watching even a couple matches can give you a decent idea of how competitive a team is
  - You can make a rough pick list simply by adding teams that you see while waiting for your match (or marking teams as do not pick)

## Match Scouting

- The best kind of data, but takes a lot of resources to collect
- People in stands watch matches and record what robots do
  - Scores
  - Penalties
  - What robots do; e.g. who's playing defense, who's breaking down, etc
  - What autonomous routines do teams use
  - Does a robot have a vulnerability to defense, like only scoring from one spot?
- You can use an automated system or paper and pencil; both do work
- AI is a great way to make a functional scouting app without knowing how to code

## Other Data Sources

- There are some automated scouting systems
- They're not a replacement for scouting but they're better than just rank
- "OPR" [thebluealliance.com](https://www.thebluealliance.com)
- "EPA" [statbotics.io](https://statbotics.io)
