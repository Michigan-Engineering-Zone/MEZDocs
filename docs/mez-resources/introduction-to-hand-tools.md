# Introduction to Hand Tools

## Introduction to Hand Tools

MEZ Teacher/Coach Workshop

## Hand Tools

- Team supervision is required, but not MEZ supervision or machine shop certification
- Please stock and label your own tools
- You can borrow things you rarely use, but please make an effort to return them
- Check and put away the tools on your table after each day

![Photo from Hand Tools](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/hand-tools.jpg)

## Measuring Devices

### Tape Measure

- Quick, easy, long, and maneuverable.
- Not sturdy or precise.

![Photo of Tape Measure](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/measuring-devices-1.jpg)


### Square

- Higher precision; doesn’t flex or bend. Better for tracing lines.
- Heavy/awkward.

![Photo of Square](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/measuring-devices-2.jpg)


### Calipers

For small, extremely precise measurements.

![Photo of Calipers](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/measuring-devices-3.jpg)


## Drills and Drivers

### Center Punch

- Makes a small divot exactly where you want to drill.
- Helps prevent the drill from sliding and significantly increases precision.

![Photo of Center Punch](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/drills-and-drivers-1.jpg)


### Powered Hand Drill

- Good for drilling holes.
- Can be used for low-torque screwing/unscrewing.

![Photo of Powered Hand Drill](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/drills-and-drivers-2.jpg)


### Impact Driver

- Not for drilling holes.
- Good for high-torque screwing/unscrewing.
- Very loud.

![Photo of Impact Driver](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/drills-and-drivers-3.jpg)


## Riveting

### Alternative to Bolts

- Much faster to go in.
- Single use, must be drilled out.
- Good for permanent installations.

![Photo of Alternative to Bolts](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/riveting-1.jpg)


### Powered Rivet Tools

- Much faster and easier.
- They will break if you use the wrong size rivet. This happens more than once per day. Please supervise your students!

![Photo of Powered Rivet Tools](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/riveting-2.jpg)


### Manual Rivet Tools

- Much more work than the powered tool.
- Useful for after the powered one breaks.
- These will break too, so again - please supervise!

![Photo of Manual Rivet Tools](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/riveting-3.jpg)


## Files and Deburrers

### File

- Smooth rough edges and corners.
- Most are intended to be used in a single direction.

![Photo of File](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/files-and-deburrers-1.jpg)


### Deburring Tool

- Can save time vs a file for long edges; great for cleaning a part after it has been cut.
- Less effective if there’s a single area that needs significant cleanup.

![Photo of Deburring Tool](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/files-and-deburrers-2.jpg)


### Flush Cutter

- Angled blade to cut something flush to its source, instead of leaving a burr.
- Please use flush cutters on all zipties. Otherwise the cut ends are sharp enough to draw blood.

![Photo of Flush Cutter](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/files-and-deburrers-3.jpg)

## Wiring Tools

### Wire Cutters

- Designed to cut metallic wire without quickly getting dull.
- Often combined with wire strippers in a single tool.

![Photo of Wire Cutters](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/wiring-tools-1.jpg)


### Wire Strippers

Designed to pull the ends of sheathing off of a run of wire.

![Photo of Wire Strippers](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/wiring-tools-2.jpg)


### Crimpers

- Crushes some kind of connector onto a wire.
- Can be generic, but also there are many designed for certain kinds of crimps.

![Photo of Crimpers](./img/introduction-to-hand-tools/wiring-tools-3.jpg)
